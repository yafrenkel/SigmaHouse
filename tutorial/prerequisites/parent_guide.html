🧑‍🏫 Manager's Guide

Everything you need to run Code Week with a light touch: how to keep her thinking for herself (not AI/Google-ing answers), how to help without giving it away, what "done" looks like each day, and how the paycheck rewards understanding — not just a working file.

🎯 The one big idea: pay for understanding, not output

You can't stop a kid from Googling or asking an AI. But you don't need to — you just make sure the money follows the thinking. The whole scheme rests on one 2-minute daily ritual:

💬 The "Explain-Back" (the anti-shortcut secret weapon)

Before she collects her understanding bonus each day, she does a 2-minute Explain-Back with you. She must do two of these:

  1. Explain one piece of her code in her own words ("what does this line do?").
  2. Predict the output of a line before running it ("what will this print?").
  3. Change it live — you request a tiny tweak and she does it in ~60 seconds (see per-day examples below).

Why this beats any AI-detector: if she copy-pasted from an AI, she can't explain it, can't predict it, and fumbles the live change. If she genuinely did the work, she'll nail all three in two minutes. Understanding can't be faked out loud.

💵 The paycheck ($50/day → $250/week)

Split the daily $50 so most of it is gated behind understanding, not just a finished file:

PartPayEarned by
Base$25Doing the day's core missions + the 👑 Boss build (the work exists and runs).
Understanding bonus$20Passing the 2-minute Explain-Back. This is the part AI can't earn for her.
Extra-mile$5A ⚡ stretch challenge, or a "no-peek" day (solved the Boss without opening the ▶ answers).

Frame it as a real job: "Base pay for doing the work. The bonus is for understanding it — because that's what you're really here to build."

🧠 Why the week is already "shortcut-resistant" by design

  • The outputs are personal/creative. Her Mad-Libs, her web page, her capstone — there's no single "answer" on Google to paste. She has to make choices.
  • There's a built-in hint path. Every puzzle has a ▶ Peek button with the answer. So she never needs to Google — the legitimate hint is right there, after she tries. (Rule of thumb we teach her: struggle 10 minutes, then Peek.)
  • The days compound. Wednesday reuses Monday/Tuesday; Friday reuses Wednesday. If she fakes an early day, the later ones won't make sense — a natural forcing function to actually learn.
  • Typing, not pasting. We ask her to type everything. Fingers learn; typos teach careful reading.

🗣️ How to talk to her about AI/Google (positive framing)

Don't make it about "cheating" or getting caught. Make it about her:

"Google and AI are amazing tools, and you'll use them your whole life. But this week they're off-limits for the puzzles — for the same reason you don't let someone else lift your weights at the gym. A muscle someone else lifts for doesn't get stronger. The struggle is the workout. Use the ▶ Peek button when you're stuck — that's your spotter."

(Using AI to explain a concept she's curious about is fine and encouraged — that's learning. Using it to hand her puzzle answers is the thing we're steering around.)

🪜 How to help when she's stuck (without giving the answer)

Use the hint ladder — climb only as high as needed:

  1. "Read the error's last line out loud. What's it saying?"
  2. "What did you expect to happen, and what happened instead?"
  3. "Which line do you think is the problem? Let's just look at that one."
  4. "Try clicking the ▶ Peek — then close it and type it from memory."

Resist typing on her keyboard. The goal is her "aha," not a working file.

🚩 Signs she's pasting instead of thinking

  • Can't explain what her own code does, or goes quiet during the Explain-Back.
  • The code is fancier than what the lesson taught (extra features/style she can't account for).
  • She finishes suspiciously fast with zero errors along the way (real coding has messy middles).
  • Fumbles a 60-second "live change."

If you see these: no drama. Just say "Cool — walk me through it" and do the live change together. If she can't, that day's understanding bonus waits until she can explain it. Keep it warm; this is coaching, not a courtroom.

🔥 Keeping her motivated (the fun engineering)

  • Move first. The pool/gym start isn't a break from learning — it primes her brain. Keep it.
  • Short bursts. ~2–2.5 hrs of coding total. When focus dips, stop. A fried kid learns nothing and hates it.
  • Be the audience. The daily show-and-tell is the real reward — your genuine "whoa, you built that?" beats the cash.
  • Let her own the Boss builds. When she picks the topic (her web page, her capstone), engagement soars.
  • Bank the streak. A visible Mission Log (5 days of checkmarks) is weirdly motivating.
  • The Friday demo is the finale — invite the family, make it an event. That pride carries into camp.

🔧 Your one prep job

For Friday's big payoff ("register a real house from the browser console"), have the smart-house hub running (python app.py) and the dashboard open. If it's not ready, the page tells her "you'll do this at camp" — so nothing breaks.

✅ Quick daily checklist for you

  1. Morning: nudge her to move, then to open the day's page.
  2. Midday: glance in once — is she typing and running things? (You don't need to hover.)
  3. End: 2-minute Explain-Back → she shows the Boss build → you pay the base + (if she passes) the understanding bonus.
  4. Cheer. Log it. Done.
