👩‍💻 Code Week: Your Coding Apprenticeship

Hi, Junior Developer! 👋 Welcome to your first paid coding job. This week you're a junior software engineer in training. Your mission: learn to read and write real code — Python and web (HTML/JavaScript) — so that by Friday you can build things that actually do stuff.

Real engineers get paid to learn and build. So do you. 💵 Do the work → get paid. Go above and beyond → earn bonuses. (See The Paycheck below.)

How each mission page works — you'll see these icons:
  • 🎯 Mission — the thing to learn.
  • 🛠 Try it — type it yourself and run it. (Do it — don't just read!)
  • ✅ Win — you nailed it. Check it off in your Mission Log.
  • 👑 Boss — the fun build at the end of the day.
  • ▶ Peek — a hint or answer, hidden until you click. Try first!

🛠 The one tool you need: Thonny

You already have Thonny (the same app used at camp). It runs regular Python right on this computer — perfect for learning. Open it, and use the big Run button (or press F5) to run your code. The bottom panel (the "Shell") is where your program talks to you.

For the web days (4 & 5) you'll also use a plain text editor and your web browser — no install needed.

A full 9-to-4 day — but only about 2–3 hours is actual coding. The rest is movement, reading, music, drawing, and a real lunch. It feels like a full workday (like a grown-up's!), but your brain stays fresh and it stays fun. 🧠

💡 Coding blocks are "work till the missions feel done," not strict clock times. The harder days (Tue–Fri) have more to sink your teeth into, so coding might run a little longer and free-choice a little shorter — totally fine. Take a break whenever your brain needs one, and if you're flying, the ⚡ stretch challenges are bottomless.

📵 Phone policy (pro mode)

Real engineers put their phones away during focus time — it's how pros concentrate. You're on the payroll now, so you get the pro treatment:

  • 🔒 Phone lives in the basket during work blocks (Coding Block 1 & Boss Build). Bonus: it keeps you honest — no sneaky Googling for answers. 😉
  • 🍔 Lunch is your phone break — check it, reply, scroll a bit.
  • 📚🚴🎨 Reading, exercise, and drawing breaks are screen-free — that's the whole point (rest your eyes, move your body, make something with your hands).
  • 🎵 Music break: phone's fine if you're using it for music.
  • 🏁 4:00 = off the clock. Your phone — and your whole evening — are all yours.

It feels strict for about a day… then it feels like a superpower. 💪

🧠 The honesty rule (this is how the bonus works):

Google and AI are awesome — but this week they're off-limits for the puzzle answers. Here's why, and it's not about getting caught: a muscle someone else lifts for you doesn't get stronger. The struggle is the workout. 🏋️

  • Stuck? Struggle for ~10 minutes, then click the ▶ Peek — that's your allowed hint, built right into every puzzle. You never need Google.
  • Each day you'll explain your code to your manager for 2 minutes to earn the Understanding bonus. If you did the thinking, this is easy money. 😎
  • Curious about why something works? Asking an AI to explain a concept is totally fine — that's learning. Asking it to hand you the answer is the thing we skip.

💡 Rules of a good coder (read once)

  • Type it, don't copy it. Your fingers learn too. Typos teach you to read carefully.
  • Errors are normal. Every coder gets red text all day. Read the last line — it usually tells you what's wrong.
  • Try before you peek. Struggling for 2 minutes teaches more than the answer does.
  • Break it on purpose. Change a number, see what happens. Curiosity is the whole job.
  • Stuck for 10 min? Take a walk, or ask for a hint. That's allowed and smart.
