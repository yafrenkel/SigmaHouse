Day 2 — Meet the Board 🤖

The goal: hold a real computer in your hand and make it obey you. Today there's no WiFi — just you, the ESP32, and Thonny. You'll make an LED blink, then read a button, then wire them together so a press flips the light.

📖 Field guide: day2.html (start at Meet your board). Want to see the electricity behind it? The LED and button circuits explain what's happening in the wires.

🎯 Missions

  • ☐ M1 · Talk to the board. Do Part 1 (the REPL). ✅ Win 2.1: you type 2 + 2 in the Shell and the board answers 4.
  • ☐ M2 · Blink an LED. Do Part 2. ✅ Win 2.2: the LED blinks once a second, running from the board.

✅ Win 2.4 (the Boss): press the button → the LED toggles and holds its new state.

🐞 Snags to watch for (and the fixes)

▶ Thonny can't find the board / no Shell

Bottom-right of Thonny → pick the interpreter MicroPython (ESP32) and the right port (e.g. COM3). If it's greyed out, close any other program using the port (Arduino IDE, PuTTY, another Thonny) and replug the USB.

▶ "Access is denied" or the connection drops

Usually the USB cable/port. Use a short, good data cable straight into a rear USB port (not a hub). Then Thonny → Stop/Restart. (This matters even more tomorrow when WiFi turns on.)

▶ Error mentioning float / __floordiv__ on a sleep

On the ESP32, use time.sleep_ms(200) (whole milliseconds) instead of time.sleep(0.2) for short delays.

▶ The button does nothing / prints 5 times

Two classics: (1) you're pressing the wrong button — this kit has two; check which pin the code uses. (2) Multiple prints per press is "bounce" — normal; the real project fixes it with a debounce, and Part 3 explains it.

💬 Explain-Back (2 min)

  • What is the REPL? How is it different from running a saved file?
  • In your blink code, what does time.sleep do — and what happens if you make the number smaller?
  • Your button uses an interrupt. In your own words, why is that better than checking the button over and over (polling)?
  • (Live change) Make the LED blink twice as fast.
